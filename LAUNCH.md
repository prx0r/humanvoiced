## 2. The winning positioning: not voice actors, just people

This is the fundamental distinction I would protect as the company grows.

# Your voice can earn.

No acting experience. No auditions with agencies. Read a short passage and create your own bookable voice profile.

I have a voice

Record a sample. Set your availability. Receive paid offers.

I need a voice

Browse real people. Pick a natural voice. Submit a script.

Traditional marketplaces select for polished professional delivery.

HumanVoiced should select for authenticity, intelligibility and reliability.

A student with a distinctive Northern English accent, an Indian engineering graduate, a retired teacher or someone with a naturally dry comedic delivery could all be valuable.

They don't need to sound like radio presenters. Their real voices are the product.

And because every narrator has their own portfolio, there is a chance for discoverability and repeat work without forcing them to bid against one another.

### Reddit strategy

I'd try r/beermoneyglobal and relevant community recruitment threads, with moderator permission, before professional casting subreddits.

Be transparent that this is an early-stage platform and that earnings depend on available work. Don't present speculative work as an existing paid vacancy.

The best referral incentive is probably a bonus after a referred person completes their first paid job, rather than paying for signups. That rewards useful supply instead of attracting fake registrations.

## 5. Pricing for ordinary people

I'd keep your platform-set pricing concept but test it against actual recording times.

| 10-minute narration        | Pilot economics |
| -------------------------- | --------------- |
| Creator pays               | $11.00          |
| Narrator receives          | $8.00           |
| Platform gross share       | $3.00           |
| Estimated recording effort | 20–40 minutes   |
| Effective narrator rate    | $12–24/hour     |

These are assumptions, not guaranteed earnings. They exclude time spent finding jobs, and the platform's $3 must cover payment processing, audio processing, support and disputes.

If ordinary narrators consistently need an hour to produce a 10-minute recording, the $11 product will need repricing.

I'd start with one fixed price, one narrator payout schedule, one correction policy. Don't introduce dynamic rarity or reputation pricing until you've observed real customer choices.

And don't start with hour-long recordings. Focus on 2–12-minute scripts, which are much easier to fulfil and review.

## 6. The first creator acquisition experiment

Search the existing freelance marketplaces for clients already purchasing recurring human narration.

Those are higher-intent prospects than randomly messaging faceless YouTube channels.

For example, the current listings I found include customers requesting four to six monthly documentary voiceovers, weekly horror narration, and bundles of philosophy videos.

Approach them through permitted contact channels with a direct comparison:

> You're already hiring someone to narrate videos. HumanVoiced lets you browse real voices, choose one, and send every future script directly to them. Fixed pricing, no auditions, and automatic audio delivery.

Don't ask them to adopt your whole agent system. Sell the next narration they already need.

## 7. The growth loop I'd design around

Ordinary person records voice → Public bookable portfolio → Creator or agent discovers voice → Paid script recording → Verified completion history → Better matching and repeat bookings → Narrator shares earnings and portfolio

The portfolio is central. Every approved person gets something worth sharing even before they receive their first order.

But the most important metric is not signups or voice samples.

It's how many approved narrators earn their first dollar within 30 days, and how many creators return to hire someone again.

A marketplace where 1,000 people sign up and nobody gets paid will die. A marketplace where 20 people consistently fulfil scripts for five recurring creators has the beginnings of a viable business.

## My recommendation

Keep the name HumanVoiced.com, subject to the unresolved registration and trademark checks. ICANN's official RDAP lookup is the next registration check; my attempts to retrieve live registry data did not establish a definitive available/taken status.

Make the first proposition:

For workers: "Your voice can earn."

For creators: "Your YouTube script, read by a real person. From $11."

For agents: "Human narration through an API."

And resist the temptation to launch translation, dubbing, advertisements, character acting, dynamic price auctions and sophisticated governance simultaneously.

Those are excellent expansion paths, but the first business is much simpler:

30 ordinary people with real voices. Five creators who need scripts read every week. A system that matches them, pays them and brings them back.

That's the wedge I'd test before anything else.
